# imagination-gym

> The training step. Encoding fails when images are faint. Imagery is a trainable skill; these are
> the reps. Grounded in mental-imagery research, not mysticism.

## When to use this

The user's palace images keep slipping, they report difficulty "seeing" anything, or they want to
strengthen the raw faculty the whole method depends on. (Note: a minority of people have aphantasia
— little or no voluntary imagery. For them, lean on verbal/spatial and motor cues instead of
insisting on pictures; the palace still works through location and story.)

## Instructions

1. **Establish a baseline.** Ask them to picture a simple object and rate vividness 1–5 (a quick
   VVIQ-style check). This tells you where to start and shows progress later.

2. **Drill detail, one sense at a time:**
   - Sight: hold an apple — colour, sheen, the dent, the stem.
   - Then add touch (weight, skin), smell, sound (the bite), taste. Stack senses gradually.

3. **Drill manipulation.** Rotate the object, change its size and colour, set it on fire, multiply
   it. Dynamic control is exactly what `loci-encoder` needs.

4. **Drill scene construction.** Build a small coherent scene from scratch (constructive episodic
   simulation): a place, a few objects, an action connecting them. This is the muscle a palace uses.

5. **Transfer to the palace.** Take a real target the user is encoding and walk them from faint to
   vivid using the above, then hand the strengthened image to `loci-encoder`.

6. **Keep sessions short and frequent.** A few minutes daily beats occasional long sessions —
   `spaced-recall` can remind them.

## Refusals

- No claims that imagery training raises IQ, unlocks "hidden brain potential", or cures conditions.
- Don't shame an aphantasic user for not seeing pictures — adapt the method, don't insist.
- Stay in the imagery-research register; never drift to spiritual-bypass language.
